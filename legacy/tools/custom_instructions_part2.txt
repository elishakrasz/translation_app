THE STYLE OF THIS TRANSLATION (meaning wins over register, register wins over flow):
1. **Literal in meaning.** The author's argument, claims, qualifications, modality ("could well argue", "perhaps") and technical terms are rendered exactly. Interpretive statements are neither softened nor sharpened; nothing is added or explained that the English does not say.
2. **Torah-scholarly in wording.** The Hebrew is written as a Hebrew-writing talmid hakham or scholar of Hasidism would write — not as a calque of English academic prose. Established concepts and expressions are preferred (ספרות חז"ל, העבודה שבלב, לימוד תורה, דרך חדשה), with the customary forms of names and honorifics (ר', הקב"ה), and rabbinic Hebrew or Aramaic phrasing where it is natural and clear. This register is deliberate, not "ornate".
3. **Literary in flow.** Natural Hebrew word order, varied sentence rhythm, cohesive transitions. English constructions carried over ("במונחים של", "באופן ש…", "מה שמעניין הוא", stacked "כאשר" clauses, passive "נעשה על ידי") are calques and are errors.
4. **Sentences may be split or merged** where Hebrew requires; this is not a fidelity error. Paragraph order and the argument's structure are preserved.
5. **Quotations from Hasidic and classical sources are given in the original Hebrew** wherever the original has been verified; those paragraphs are the source text itself and are outside the scope of fidelity review. Quotations not yet verified are translated in the Hebrew style of Hasidic literature (אמר, והנה, דהיינו) — that register is deliberate.
6. **No added glosses.** Nothing is explained that the English does not explain. The author's own glosses may be kept or dropped when they would merely repeat a Hebrew word (e.g. "tikkun (a fixing)").
7. The author's English metaphors are kept. Yiddish words are kept in Yiddish (in Hebrew letters). Neither is an error.
8. "Piety" is יראת שמים and "pious" ירא שמים — never חסידות/חסיד, which denote the Hasidic movement and its followers (deliberate wordplay on חסידות is the only exception).
9. Terminology follows the decided-terms list and the paragraph's required terms exactly (inflected as grammar requires).
10. Unvocalized text; niqqud only where a decided term carries it.
11. **Names of God:** א־לוהים, א־לוהות, א־לוהי; ה' in fixed phrases; הקב"ה in set phrases — never אלוהים, אלוהות or האל in translated text (decided 2026-10-05; see decided-terms).
Base register: Rav Adin Steinsaltz — clear explanatory Hebrew whose rhythm unpacks the meaning, as if the material were being taught. Full spelling (כתיב מלא).
